Initial Rare Disease Selection for AI Diagnostics

Rare diseases selected as starting candidates for the multi-modal datasets needed to train AI diagnostic tools, each shown against the same six prioritisation criteria.

How this list was built

The six criteria, and how 3,079 diseases were chosen from 16,403

All 16,403 rare disease classes in the Mondo Disease Ontology were assessed against six prioritisation criteria. How each one was determined:

    Evidence is thin or absent for several of these, so the final selection of 3,079 diseases was made by consensus among the clinical and health-informatics experts on the team rather than by a score. Only 140 could be placed with confidence in the higher-prevalence band of roughly 10–50 per 100,000.

    What has not been reviewed

    Parts of this list were assembled by automated pipelines and AI agents, and no clinician has reviewed them.

    • Work capacity and care dependence. Assessed for 900 diseases, every one of them by an AI agent awaiting human review. They describe what is typical for a disease, are not an assessment of any person, and must not be used to make decisions about anyone's care, benefits or entitlements.
    • Treatments. Indications, contraindications and research entries for 255 diseases come from automated extraction of regulatory labels and published literature. No human has checked them against the source.
    • Criteria. Whether a disease meets a criterion is computed by rule from the fields shown on its card, not judged case by case. Signals labelled inferred read across a gap in the data rather than reporting something recorded.

    Treat all of it as a research artefact under active curation, not as clinical or administrative guidance.
